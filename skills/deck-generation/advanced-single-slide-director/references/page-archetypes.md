# Page Archetypes

Select the semantic job before selecting a layout. A page may contain several content types, but it must have one dominant archetype.

| Archetype | Use when | Preferred visual strategy | Default silhouette | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| problem-diagnosis | Defining the current problem, failure mode, dependency, or unmet need | Fact-based causal diagram, sourced pain metric, honest conceptual scene | One dominant problem statement with a short causal chain | Showing the complete solution too early |
| principle | Explaining a technical mechanism or causal logic | Grounded mechanism diagram or conceptual sectional view | One dominant mechanism with two or three callouts | Icon rows and fake laboratory imagery |
| validation | Explaining confirmed performance or feasibility | Exact-value comparison chart or result hierarchy | Result on one side, interpretation and boundary on the other | Untraceable gauges, fake reports, or invented dashboards |
| evidence-wall | Summarizing several independent proof categories in text | Fact-led proof summary with clear category hierarchy | One dominant conclusion plus a structured supporting rail | Fake document thumbnails or many equal cards |
| application-case | Explaining a confirmed use case or customer value | Process, before/after logic, scenario diagram, or non-documentary concept visual | One large scenario logic with a compact result block | Generated customer photos, fake sites, or product evidence |
| commercial-proof | Explaining confirmed traction, order, revenue, or partnership | Exact metrics, progression, or relationship diagram | Metric-led hierarchy supported by concise sourced statements | Fake contracts, coins, handshakes, or growth-arrow decoration |
| market-analysis | Explaining opportunity, segment, or competitor position | Sourced chart, comparison matrix, or segment map | One chart or matrix plus a takeaway | A dashboard made from many small cards |
| solution-system | Explaining what the solution contains and how it works | Grounded architecture, process, or conceptual system view | System as the center with a clear flow | A row of unrelated feature icons or a fake product photograph |
| roadmap | Explaining chronological execution or milestones | Confirmed dates, deliverables, and stage progression | One clear progression with milestone emphasis | Repeated cards with no causal sequence |
| team-backing | Demonstrating ability to execute | Role-capability map and confirmed achievements in text | A small number of meaningful profiles or capability groups | Synthetic portraits, cartoon avatars, or decorative role icons |
| content-close | Synthesizing confirmed value, implementation, roadmap, metrics, or next steps before the ending | Strong synthesis, progression, or symbolic thematic visual | One dominant synthesis with concise value and action | Empty thank-you art or equal cards |
| summary-close | Resolving the presentation on the final slide | One symbolic thematic visual that carries emotion, plus one memorable conclusion | Cover-level emotional close with large whitespace and at most one short support line | New evidence, roadmaps, dense cards, charts, process diagrams, fake documentary imagery, or generic thank-you art |

For cover pages, use a minimal project-identifying composition. Do not apply a body-page archetype mechanically.
