# Page Archetypes

Select the semantic job before selecting a layout. A page may contain several content types, but it must have one dominant archetype.

**Every archetype below carries an illustration area range.** Area is not optional: a page with no dominant visual reads as a text dump regardless of how good the copy is.

**Every "Avoid" is paired with "Instead use".** A prohibition with no replacement is the reason pages collapse into text-only layouts.

| Archetype | Use when | Preferred visual strategy | Default silhouette | Avoid | Instead use | Illustration area |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| problem-diagnosis | Defining the current problem, failure mode, dependency, or unmet need | Fact-based causal diagram, sourced pain metric, honest conceptual scene | One dominant problem statement with a short causal chain | Showing the complete solution too early; icon rows standing in for the problem | Draw the failing mechanism itself: the broken link, the bottleneck, the overloaded node, with named parts | 45–60% |
| principle | Explaining a technical mechanism or causal logic | Grounded mechanism diagram or conceptual sectional view | One dominant mechanism with two or three callouts | Icon rows and fake laboratory imagery | A cross-section or cutaway with 5–7 real named components; a clearly illustrative technical drawing, not a photo | 50–68% |
| validation | Explaining confirmed performance or feasibility | Exact-value comparison chart or result hierarchy | Result on one side, interpretation and boundary on the other | Untraceable gauges, fake reports, or invented dashboards | A clean chart drawn only from confirmed values, with direct labels; the chart *is* the illustration | 45–60% |
| evidence-wall | Summarizing several independent proof categories in text | Fact-led proof summary with clear category hierarchy | One dominant conclusion plus a structured supporting rail | Fake document thumbnails or many equal cards | A relationship diagram showing how the categories connect, plus one dominant conclusion — draw the *structure* of the proof, never fake artifacts | 35–50% |
| application-case | Explaining a confirmed use case or customer value | Process, before/after logic, scenario diagram, or non-documentary concept visual | One large scenario logic with a compact result block | Generated customer photos, fake sites, or product evidence | A before/after split or a scenario flow drawn as a clearly illustrative scene with simplified figures and no real locations | 50–70% |
| commercial-proof | Explaining confirmed traction, order, revenue, or partnership | Exact metrics, progression, or relationship diagram | Metric-led hierarchy supported by concise sourced statements | Fake contracts, coins, handshakes, or growth-arrow decoration | A progression or relationship diagram from the confirmed numbers, with the largest metric as the visual anchor | 40–55% |
| market-analysis | Explaining opportunity, segment, or competitor position | Sourced chart, comparison matrix, or segment map | One chart or matrix plus a takeaway | A dashboard made from many small cards | One dominant chart or a 2×2 matrix at 50%+ with a narrow insight rail beside it | 50–70% |
| solution-system | Explaining what the solution contains and how it works | Grounded architecture, process, or conceptual system view | System as the center with a clear flow | A row of unrelated feature icons or a fake product photograph | One annotated system view with named components and directional connectors | 50–70% |
| roadmap | Explaining chronological execution or milestones | Confirmed dates, deliverables, and stage progression | One clear progression with milestone emphasis | Repeated cards with no causal sequence | A single horizontal or vertical timeline with real dates and one highlighted milestone, with a symbolic end-state visual at the far end | 40–55% |
| team-backing | Demonstrating ability to execute | Role-capability map and confirmed achievements in text | A small number of meaningful profiles or capability groups | Synthetic portraits, cartoon avatars, or decorative role icons | A capability map: roles as named groups connected to the confirmed achievements, drawn as a clearly illustrative diagram with no faces | 35–50% |
| content-close | Synthesizing confirmed value, implementation, roadmap, metrics, or next steps before the ending | Strong synthesis, progression, or symbolic thematic visual | One dominant synthesis with concise value and action | Empty thank-you art or equal cards | One symbolic thematic illustration built from the project's own subject matter, carrying the synthesis in a single line | 45–60% |
| summary-close | Resolving the presentation on the final slide | One symbolic thematic visual that carries emotion, plus one memorable conclusion | Cover-level emotional close with large whitespace and at most one short support line | New evidence, roadmaps, dense cards, charts, process diagrams, fake documentary imagery, or generic thank-you art | One cover-level symbolic visual at 35–60%, large whitespace, and a single memorable conclusion line | 35–60% |

## Cover pages

Use a minimal project-identifying composition with a strong thematic visual. **Illustration area 55–100%, bleeding off at least two edges** (full bleed is preferred). Keep copy sparse and leave deliberate title space. Do not fabricate a recognizable institution facade or sign — build the composition from the project's own subject matter (its objects, environment, or mechanism).

Do not apply a body-page archetype mechanically.

## Chart pages

A chart page's `visual_strategy` is `fact-based-chart` and its illustration area is the chart's own area (45–70%). **Do not add a decorative illustration to a chart page** — on these pages the chart is the illustration. Never invent legends, values, or decorative data points.
