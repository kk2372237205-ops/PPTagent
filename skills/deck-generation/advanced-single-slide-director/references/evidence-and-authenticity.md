# Evidence And Authenticity

## Separate Facts From Visuals

- Uploaded source files are a factual text bank. Their images may be read for OCR or semantic recovery only when ordinary parsing is insufficient.
- Uploaded source images are not an artwork library. Do not extract, crop, mask, embed, beautify, or pass them to Image2 as slide content.
- The palette reference is the only user image used as a visual constraint, and it controls color relationships only.
- A generated visual explains an idea. It never proves that an institution, product, award, experiment, customer, or result exists.

## Choose A Visual Strategy

- Use fact-based charts, timelines, processes, comparisons, or system diagrams when confirmed text contains enough structure.
- Use conceptual illustration for themes, mechanisms, problems, opportunities, and emotional framing when it can remain clearly non-documentary.
- Use typography and restrained geometry when the facts do not justify a detailed visual.
- Prefer one dominant information hierarchy. It may contain several generated visual units when each one supports a different stage, claim, comparison, mechanism, context, or result in the same reading path.
- Keep every label, value, date, and relationship traceable to the confirmed content package.

## Integrate Text And Generated Visuals

- Treat generated visuals as parts of the final slide composition, not as assets that will be inserted later.
- Bind every visual unit to the text or conclusion it supports. Use sequence, cause, contrast, mechanism, context, result, or evidence relationships.
- Let semantic relationships determine placement. A timeline image belongs at its milestone; comparison imagery belongs with its side; a mechanism view belongs with its explanation.
- Do not reserve one fixed media zone for every page. Allow asymmetric, full-width, diagonal, staggered, layered, or background compositions when they preserve readability.
- Multiple visuals must behave as one story, not an unrelated photo collage. Keep a clear primary-secondary hierarchy.

## Prevent Fabrication

- Do not ask Image2 to recreate, beautify, redraw, or simulate a certificate, contract, report, patent page, test record, official screenshot, invoice, order, award, dashboard, or news coverage.
- Do not generate readable school or institution signs, official logos, product labels, or documentary interfaces unless that exact visible text is explicitly confirmed and the result cannot be mistaken for proof.
- Do not portray a synthetic campus, laboratory, factory, customer site, product, person, or experiment as a real photographed scene.
- A clearly conceptual or editorial scene may provide context, mechanism, atmosphere, or a placeholder-like communication cue when it is not presented as factual proof.
- For a confirmed metric, Image2 may draw a clean chart only from the exact labels and values in the render contract. Missing values stay omitted.
- For a cover, create a strong thematic visual with deliberate title whitespace. Prefer symbolic subject matter over a fictional branded building or fake documentary scene.
- When no grounded diagram or honest conceptual visual is appropriate, use typography and neutral geometry. State factual limitations in warnings instead of fabricating proof.

## Control Generic UI Language

- Generic decorative icon count is zero by default for competitions, technical reports, research reports, government presentations, investor roadshows, and formal business proposals.
- Permit a functional icon only when it clarifies a compact process and no clearer typographic or geometric treatment exists.
- Do not represent a person, date, location, certificate, security claim, product feature, experiment, customer, patent, award, or commercial result with a stock icon.
- Do not use repeated pills, badges, buttons, or equal-weight cards as the main composition.
